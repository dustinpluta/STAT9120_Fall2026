## Catalog Course Description

This course is a study of the general linear statistical model. Topics include the theory of linear models, distributions of quadratic forms, full rank linear models, estimation under linear constraints and hypothesis testing for the full rank linear models, and fixed effect models of less than full rank, and other related models.

**Prerequisite:** STAT 7630 Applied Linear Models

## Course Objectives

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Use matrix and vector-space methods to formulate and analyze general linear models.
2. Apply properties of symmetric, idempotent, projection, and generalized inverse matrices in linear model theory.
3. Derive and characterize the distributions of linear and quadratic forms of normal random variables.
4. Derive least-squares estimators and establish their statistical properties under the general linear model.
5. Distinguish between full-rank and less-than-full-rank linear models and explain the consequences of rank deficiency.
6. Determine whether linear functions of model parameters are estimable and construct estimators of estimable functions.
7. Obtain least-squares estimators subject to linear constraints and understand the relationship between constraints, identifiability, and estimability.
8. Formulate and test general linear hypotheses using quadratic forms and associated (F)-tests.
9. Construct and interpret confidence intervals and confidence regions for linear functions of model parameters.
10. Apply general linear model theory to fixed-effects models and related model formulations.

### Homework Assignments

Homework assignments will emphasize the theoretical development of linear models and will consist primarily of mathematical derivations, proofs, and applications of matrix and distribution theory. Some assignments may also include computational problems intended to illustrate theoretical results.

Due dates will typically be 1–2 weeks after an assignment is given.

Late assignments will not be accepted without prior approval.

Submitted assignments should be clearly organized, with the assignment number, student name, and date clearly visible on the first page.

All derivations and supporting arguments should be presented clearly. Important intermediate steps should be included rather than only a final result.

Any results, derivations, or other work included that is not the student's own must be cited with a complete reference.

Typed assignments using LaTeX or Markdown are strongly preferred.

Homework submission will occur online. See the course webpage for further instructions.

### Midterm Exam

The midterm examination will be administered in class.

The examination will emphasize mathematical and statistical reasoning and will include questions similar in style and difficulty to homework assignments and examples developed in lecture.

Specific material covered and further details regarding the examination format will be provided in class.

### Final Exam

The final examination will be cumulative, with an emphasis on material covered after the midterm.

Students will be expected to demonstrate an integrated understanding of the theory of general linear models, including matrix methods, estimation, distributions of quadratic forms, estimability, and hypothesis testing.

Specific details regarding the examination format will be provided in class.
